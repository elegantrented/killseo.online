Refinancing a car loan means replacing your current loan with a new one, ideally with a lower rate or a payment that fits your budget better. It can save real money, but not in every case.

## When refinancing can help

- **Your credit has improved** since you took the original loan.
- **Rates have dropped** compared with when you bought the car.
- **You took dealer financing** without shopping around.
- **You need a lower monthly payment** and accept a longer term.

## When it may not be worth it

- You are near the end of the loan, when most interest is already paid.
- The car is worth less than you owe (negative equity), which limits your options.
- Fees on the new loan cancel out the savings.
- A longer term lowers the payment but raises the total interest you pay.

## How to compare

1. Find your current rate, payoff amount and remaining term.
2. Get quotes from a few lenders, including a credit union.
3. Compare the **total cost** over the life of each loan, not just the monthly payment.
4. Check for prepayment penalties on your current loan.
