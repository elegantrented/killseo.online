"Lease maturity" is the date your lease term ends. In the months before it, the leasing company will start sending letters about turn-in inspections and your options. It pays to decide early rather than in the last week.

## Your three main options

- **Return the car.** You schedule an inspection, pay any excess mileage or wear charges, and walk away.
- **Buy the car out.** You pay the purchase price in your contract (the residual plus fees and tax), with cash or a **lease payoff loan**.
- **Trade it in.** A dealer pays off the lease and applies any difference to your next car. If the car is worth more than the payoff, that difference is equity you should be paid for.

## How to tell if a buyout is worth it

Compare two numbers: your **payoff amount** (ask the leasing company for it) and the car's **current market value** (check several pricing guides and real listings). If the market value is higher, a buyout or a trade-in captures that equity. If it is lower, returning the car usually costs less.

Mileage matters too. If you are far over your allowance, the per-mile charges at return can be large, and buying the car makes them disappear.

## Timing

Start about 60–90 days before maturity. That gives you time to get a payoff quote, shop financing, and book an inspection if you decide to return the car. Waiting until the last few days limits your choices.
