A low monthly payment does not automatically mean a good lease. To judge a lease deal you need to look at the numbers behind the payment.

## The numbers that matter

- **Capitalized cost:** the price of the car in the lease. Negotiate it like a purchase price.
- **Money factor:** the lease's interest rate. Multiply by 2,400 to get a rough APR equivalent.
- **Residual value:** what the car is expected to be worth at lease end. A higher residual usually means lower payments.
- **Due at signing:** down payment, first month, fees and taxes. Large amounts down make the monthly payment look smaller.
- **Mileage allowance:** make sure it matches how much you really drive.

## How to compare offers fairly

Add up **everything you will pay over the lease**: amount due at signing plus all monthly payments. Divide by the number of months. That "effective monthly cost" lets you compare offers with different down payments.

## Common traps

- Payments quoted with a big down payment hidden in the fine print
- Low mileage limits that lead to overage charges
- Add-ons rolled into the lease without being asked for
- Acquisition and disposition fees you did not account for

## Timing

End of month, end of quarter and model-year changeovers are often good times to find incentives, but the right deal depends on the specific car.
