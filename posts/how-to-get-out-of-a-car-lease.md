Life changes: a move, a new job, a growing family. Leases are built to run their full term, so ending one early takes some planning. Here are the main routes.

## 1. Transfer the lease (lease takeover)

Someone else takes over your remaining payments and the car. Not every leasing company allows it, and some charge a transfer fee or keep you liable if the new driver defaults. Read your contract or call the leasing company first.

## 2. Buy the car out early

Most leases let you buy the car before the term ends. The early payoff is usually higher than the end-of-term residual, but if the car has gained value it can still make sense, and you can sell it yourself afterward.

## 3. Sell or trade it to a dealer

A dealer pays off the lease and takes the car. If the car is worth more than the payoff, you may receive the difference. Some leasing companies restrict third-party payoffs, so check first.

## 4. Early termination

Returning the car early through the leasing company is usually the most expensive route, because you may owe a large share of the remaining payments plus fees.

## What to ask before choosing

- What is my early payoff amount today?
- Does my leasing company allow transfers or third-party buyouts?
- What fees apply to each option?
- Am I still liable after a transfer?
