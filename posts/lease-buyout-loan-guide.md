When a car lease ends you usually have two choices: hand the car back, or buy it. A **lease buyout loan** is simply the financing that lets you do the second one without paying the full price in cash.

## What you are actually buying

Every lease contract lists a **residual value**, the price the leasing company agreed to sell you the car for at the end of the term. Your buyout amount is normally that residual plus any fees and taxes your state charges. The leasing company can give you an exact payoff quote; ask for it in writing.

## When a buyout makes sense

- **The car is worth more than the buyout price.** Used-car values move, and a residual set three years ago can end up below what the car would sell for today.
- **You are over your mileage limit.** Buying the car means you are not charged per-mile overage fees at turn-in.
- **You know the car's history.** You have driven it since new, which is more than you can say about most used cars.
- **You would face wear-and-tear charges** for dents, tires or interior damage on return.

If the car is worth well under the buyout price and in great shape, returning it is often the better deal.

## How the financing works

A lease buyout loan works much like a used-car loan: a lender pays the leasing company, the title moves into your name, and you repay the lender over a set term. Rates and terms depend on your credit, the car and the lender, so compare offers before you sign.

The paperwork is where people get stuck: payoff letters, title transfer, registration and sales tax all have to line up. Some companies specialise in handling that part for you.
