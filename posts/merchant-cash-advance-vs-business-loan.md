When a business needs money quickly, for inventory, payroll or a repair, the options that come up first are usually a **merchant cash advance** or a short-term **working capital loan**. They can look similar, but they work differently.

## Merchant cash advance (MCA)

An MCA is not technically a loan. A funder gives you a lump sum in exchange for a share of your future sales. You repay through a fixed percentage of daily or weekly card sales or bank deposits until an agreed total is paid back.

- **Pros:** fast decisions, flexible credit requirements, payments that shrink when sales slow.
- **Cons:** the cost is quoted as a "factor rate", which can be far more expensive than a loan once you convert it to an annual rate.

## Working capital loan

A working capital loan is a short-term loan with a set repayment schedule and an interest rate.

- **Pros:** usually cheaper than an MCA, with clearer terms.
- **Cons:** stricter qualification and fixed payments even in slow months.

## How to compare offers

- Ask for the **total payback amount**, not just the payment.
- Work out the approximate annual cost so you can compare offers fairly.
- Check for prepayment terms: does paying early save you anything?
- Read the contract for personal guarantees and what happens if sales drop.
