Personal loans and student loan refinancing are two of the most common ways people restructure debt. Both can save money, and both have trade-offs worth understanding first.

## Personal loans

A personal loan is an unsecured, fixed-rate loan repaid in equal monthly payments. People use them to consolidate credit card debt, cover a large expense or fund a home project.

**What affects your rate:** credit score, income, existing debt and the loan term. Shorter terms usually mean lower total interest.

**Watch for:** origination fees, which reduce the amount you actually receive.

## Student loan refinancing

Refinancing replaces one or more student loans with a new private loan, ideally at a lower rate.

**It can make sense if** your credit and income have improved since you borrowed and you have private loans or high-rate debt.

**Be careful with federal loans.** Refinancing federal student loans into a private loan means giving up federal protections such as income-driven repayment, deferment and forgiveness programs. That decision cannot be undone.

## How to compare lenders

- Check your rate with a soft credit pull where offered.
- Compare APRs, not just interest rates.
- Look at fees, autopay discounts and hardship options.
- Compare the total cost over the full term.
