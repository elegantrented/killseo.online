**Revenue-based financing** gives a business money now in exchange for a share of future revenue. It has become a common option for businesses with steady sales that do not fit a bank's lending criteria.

## How it works

A funder provides a lump sum. You repay it as a percentage of your revenue, often through automatic withdrawals, until you reach an agreed total. When sales are strong you repay faster; when they dip, payments can shrink, depending on the agreement.

## Compared with a traditional loan

- **Approval:** usually based more on revenue than on credit or collateral.
- **Speed:** often faster than a bank loan.
- **Cost:** often higher. The cost may be quoted as a fixed fee or factor rate rather than interest, so convert it to an approximate annual rate to compare fairly.
- **Payments:** may flex with sales, whereas loan payments are fixed.

## Good fits and poor fits

**Good fit:** businesses with consistent revenue that need money for growth, inventory or a seasonal push.

**Poor fit:** businesses with thin margins, where a large share of every sale going to repayment can squeeze cash flow.

## Questions to ask

- What is the total payback amount?
- How are payments calculated and how often are they taken?
- Is there a discount for early payoff?
- What happens if revenue drops sharply?
